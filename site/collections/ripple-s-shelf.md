Transcripts of episodes 1-225 of the Bu Mingbai Podcast (不明白) co-founded by New York Times columnist Li Yuan and Chinese journalists working for Western media organizations in May 2022. The literal translation of the Chinese name is “I Don’t Understand.” The podcast aims to provide uncensored interviews with experts and ordinary Chinese about current affairs (and history) in China. It was blocked in China from the second episode on.  People in 199 countries have listened to the podcast.

The podcast's name refers to the letter of admonition issued by the Wuhan Police Bureau signed Dr Li Wenliang, the COVID “whistleblower.” The doctor had warned his medical colleagues about SARS-like pneumonia cases at his hospital (the first COVID cases) in a private WeChat group. On the letter of admonition, Dr. Li was required to write “(I) can” and “(I) understand.” Shortly after the police document was published online, the phrases “(I) can’t” and “(I) don’t understand” were used by people as a form of resistance, as well as an apt description of their everyday lives, so those two phrases were largely blocked on Chinese internet. The podcast was launched shortly after Dr. Li died of COVID. The government portrayed Dr. Li as a national hero after he died.

The Bumingbai Podcast began in the midst of China’s harsh COVID-19 lockdowns in 2022. Under the dogma of the Zero-COVID policy, authorities imposed indiscriminate lockdowns, leaving citizens hungry and, in some cases, unable to receive timely medical care, resulting in deaths. Small business owners were driven into bankruptcy and forced onto the streets. Many Chinese despaired, asking, "How did my country come to this?" Even Yuan Li struggled to answer. This led to the creation of the Bumingbai Podcast—'bumingbai' means 'I have no idea' in Chinese. Yes! And it also references the document Dr. Li Wenliang had to sign in that same context. I think "I don’t understand” is a better translation.
